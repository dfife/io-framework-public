    The primitive reduced baryonic observable O_b(c) is a 1-form transfer observable.

III. Gap 2: 1-form observables couple to dV/dalpha
==================================================

Theorem 18.18 (uniqueness of the reduced first-order gauge covector)
    On the one-dimensional reduced gauge manifold M_g,red = { alpha }, any local natural
    first-order central 1-form observable built from the gauge modular scalar

        V(alpha) = -2 ln(cos alpha)

    is proportional to

        dV.

Proof

        Omega[V] = F(alpha, V, dV/dalpha) d alpha.

    Naturality and first-order linearity in the perturbation imply linear dependence on the first
    jet of V. Therefore

        Omega[V] = c(alpha, V) (dV/dalpha) d alpha.

    Reparameterization covariance on the one-dimensional reduced gauge line removes any dependence
    on the chosen coordinate description beyond the scalar V itself, so the only natural covector
    built from V at first order is

        c dV

    for some scalar normalization c.

    The normalization is fixed by the primitive response definition: the baryonic observable is the
    first variation of the gauge background, so it must be the exact differential of the reduced
    gauge potential rather than an arbitrary rescaling of it.

    Hence

        Omega[V] = dV.

QED.

Corollary 18.19 (scalar coefficient of the 1-form response)
    In the alpha coordinate,

        dV = V'(alpha) d alpha

    with

        V'(alpha) = 2 gamma.

Proof
    Since

        gamma = tan alpha,
        V(alpha) = -2 ln(cos alpha),

    one computes

        V'(alpha) = 2 tan alpha = 2 gamma.

QED.

Why not V or V''?
    The theorem excludes them for structural reasons:

    - V is a 0-form scalar, not a 1-form covector.
    - V'' belongs to the second jet / Hessian class and corresponds to the 2-form-curvature sector

    The reduced baryonic observable is first-order and covector-valued, so it couples to dV and
    therefore to V', not to V or V''.

    So only V' has the correct first-order odd response behavior.

IV. Gap 3: the 1-form class scales exactly as x^(-1)
==================================================

Definition 18.20 (self-similar radial transfer curves)
    Let c_r be the family of homologous radial transfer curves in the Schwarzschild interior,
    normalized so that their characteristic proper length is proportional to the radial scale r.

    In particular,

        length(c_{r_s}) / length(c_{R_U}) = r_s / R_U = x.

Theorem 18.21 (line-scaling theorem for the 1-form class)
    Let omega be a reduced 1-form transfer datum whose scalar coefficient is constant on each
    self-similar radial curve c_r. Then

        integral_{c_{R_U}} omega
        = (R_U / r_s) integral_{c_{r_s}} omega
        = x^(-1) integral_{c_{r_s}} omega.

Proof
    By definition of a 1-form transfer observable, the primitive quantity is its line integral.

    On the self-similar family c_r, the reduced scalar coefficient is constant on each c_r, so the
    integral is coefficient times curve length. Therefore

        integral_{c_r} omega propto length(c_r).

    Taking the ratio between observer and horizon scales gives

        integral_{c_{R_U}} omega / integral_{c_{r_s}} omega
        = length(c_{R_U}) / length(c_{r_s})
        = R_U / r_s
        = 1 / x.

QED.

Comment
    This is exact for the 1-form class because the primitive observable is a line integral. It is
    not the scaling law for the scalar transfer class of GTTP and not the scaling law for the
    plaquette / curvature class.
