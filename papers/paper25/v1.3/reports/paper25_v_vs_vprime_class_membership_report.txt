Can the IO stack explain, at theorem grade, why some observables read V(alpha)=K_gauge while first-variation observables read V'(alpha)=2 gamma?

Bottom line

Challenge To The Current Heuristic
---------------------------------

Claude's heuristic 'finite operations read V, infinitesimal ones read V'' is close, but too loose for theorem grade.

Why it is too loose:

1. In the first-law / relative-entropy setting, infinitesimally nearby states still read the fixed modular Hamiltonian K of the reference state.
2. V' appears when the physical construction itself is a derivative of an alpha-parameterized transport family, or when one compares different modular flows via a relative Hamiltonian/cocycle.
3. Therefore the correct dichotomy is not simply finite versus infinitesimal. It is fixed modular-readout versus variational/relative-modular response.

Where The Split Actually Lives In H_IO
-------------------------------------

Best current answer:

1. In Z_g = W*(K_hat_g), the reduced state gives one distinguished modular generator K_hat_g = V I.
2. Paper 16 degree bookkeeping only says how many active transfer slots survive. It does not by itself choose V over V', because both are compatible with one-slot degree-1 grammar.
3. Paper 18.B identifies the variational/tangent grammar explicitly: first variation of transport lands on dV.
4. Papers 22-23 show the real bottleneck is observable class-membership: does the physical readout stay in the fixed modular-readout class, or does it live in the derivative/relative class, or even a two-slot self-intensity class?

So:

- primary location of the distinction: modular theory plus relative-modular/derivation grammar
- secondary location: bridge typing and scalarization-preserves-type hypothesis
- not the primary location: Paper 16 degree theorem alone

Candidate Theorem
-----------------

Conditional modular-versus-variational class-membership theorem

Premises:
- M_red = M_th tensor Z_g with Z_g = W*(K_hat_g).
- omega_red is a faithful normal reduced state with modular flow sigma_t.
- On the active gauge eigensector, K_hat_g(alpha) = V(alpha) I.
- The physical observable family O is certified either as a modular-readout object or as a variational/relative object.
- Scalarization preserves observable type whenever a scalar coefficient is extracted.

Conclusions:
- If O is a positive one-slot transfer observable acted on by the fixed modular flow of omega_red, then its logarithmic coefficient is V(alpha), not V'(alpha).
- If O is the first variation of a transport family or a relative-state intertwiner, then the linear coefficient is dK_hat_g/dalpha = V'(alpha) I.

Interpretation:
- WMR closes if the weak-rate observable is proved to lie in the modular-readout class.
- The PSRP numerator closes if the seed covariance is proved to be a center-generated modular readout rather than a first-variation response or same-fiber self-intensity.

Source Index
------------

- Herman-Takesaki 1970: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01646631
  role: For a faithful normal state, automorphisms commuting with the modular/KMS group leave the state invariant; the paper also treats KMS structure for quasi-free automorphisms of the CAR algebra.
- Araki 1971: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/kyotoms1969/6/3/6_3_385/_article/-char/en
  role: Quasifree states are characterized as the unique KMS state of a Bogoliubov automorphism of CAR.
- Bisognano-Wichmann 1976: https://doi.org/10.1063/1.522898
  role: Canonical AQFT source for the statement that a specific modular automorphism group is tied to a specific geometric one-parameter flow for wedge algebras.
- Araki 1973: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/kyotoms1969/9/1/9_1_165/_article
  role: Relative Hamiltonian / intertwiner formalism: when one compares two faithful normal states, the natural object is a relative modular cocycle between their modular groups.
- Derezinski-Jaksic-Pillet 2001: https://www.maphysto.dk/publications/MPS-RR/2001/45.pdf
  role: Perturbing the dynamics by Q produces a perturbed dynamics and a new perturbed KMS state; changing the generator changes the equilibrium state.
- Blanco-Casini 2013: https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.1121
  role: For infinitesimally different states, the first-law relation uses the expectation of the modular Hamiltonian of the reference state: first-order variation does not by itself imply a derivative of the modular generator.
- Faulkner-Leigh-Parrikar-Wang 2016: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/JHEP09(2016)038
  role: First-order changes of modular Hamiltonians arise when the region/family is deformed; the derivative belongs to the deformed family, not to the fixed modular flow itself.
