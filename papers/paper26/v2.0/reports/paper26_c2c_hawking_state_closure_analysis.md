### Lemma 26.C2c.L1: Fixed-Carrier Hawking/KMS Covariance Uniqueness

On a fixed finite-dimensional bosonic one-particle carrier `E_bdy`, with
self-adjoint positive one-particle Hamiltonian `h_bdy` and fixed inverse
temperature `beta_H`, there is a unique gauge-invariant quasi-free CCR KMS
state for the dynamics generated by `h_bdy`. Its one-particle covariance is

```text
G_H^(1) = (exp(beta_H h_bdy) - I)^(-1).
```

Proof. This is the standard Araki-Woods / Bratteli-Robinson characterization of
gauge-invariant quasi-free KMS states on a bosonic CCR algebra. For fixed
`h_bdy` and fixed `beta_H`, the Bose occupation function is fixed mode by mode.
Since `E_bdy` is finite-dimensional in the `ell=1` shell, no domain or trace
class complication enters the shell-level statement. Therefore any
gauge-invariant quasi-free KMS covariance on this carrier must equal
`(exp(beta_H h_bdy)-I)^(-1)`. Conversely, this covariance defines the
gauge-invariant quasi-free KMS state for the fixed dynamics.

Chain: Standard CCR/KMS theorem (Araki-Woods quasi-free CCR KMS uniqueness;
Bratteli-Robinson, Operator Algebras and Quantum Statistical Mechanics, Vol. 2,
KMS/quasi-free state sections) + fixed one-particle Hamiltonian on `E_bdy` +
fixed inverse temperature `beta_H`.

Status: `DERIVED/THEOREM`.

### Lemma 26.C2c.L2: Hawking Exponent on the C2.2 Boundary Carrier

For the `S^2 ell=1` coexact shell on a Schwarzschild horizon of radius `r_s`,
the coexact Laplacian eigenvalue is

```text
lambda_1 = 2 / r_s^2.
```

The corresponding massless boundary mode frequency is

```text
omega_1 = c sqrt(lambda_1) = sqrt(2) c / r_s.
```

With Schwarzschild Hawking inverse temperature

```text
beta_H = 4 pi r_s / (hbar c),
```

the dimensionless thermal exponent is

```text
beta_H hbar omega_1 = 4 pi sqrt(2).
```

Therefore the unique fixed-carrier KMS occupation is

```text
g_H(ell=1) = 1 / (exp(4 pi sqrt(2)) - 1).
```

Proof. The coexact `S^2` shell eigenvalue is fixed by the Hodge spectrum on a
round sphere. The mode frequency follows from the massless wave relation
`omega=c sqrt(lambda)`. Hawking's Schwarzschild temperature gives
`T_H = hbar c/(4 pi k_B r_s)`, hence
`beta_H = 1/(k_B T_H)=4 pi r_s/(hbar c)`. Multiplication gives
`beta_H hbar omega_1 = 4 pi sqrt(2)`. Substitution into Lemma 26.C2c.L1 gives
the stated occupation.

Chain: Paper 26 v2.0 Lemma C2.2 carrier identification + standard Hodge
spectrum on round `S^2` + massless wave dispersion on the boundary carrier +
Hawking 1975 Schwarzschild temperature.

Status: `DERIVED/THEOREM`.

### Lemma 26.C2c.L3: Isometric Transport Preserves the KMS Covariance

Let `U_coex:E_bdy -> E_br` be the unitary/isometric identification from
Definition 26.C2c.D1. If the bridge-shell Hamiltonian is transported by

```text
h_bdy = U_coex^* h_br U_coex,
```

then the unique gauge-invariant quasi-free KMS covariance transports by

```text
G_bdy^(1) = U_coex^* G_br^(1) U_coex.
```

Proof. Functional calculus commutes with unitary conjugation:

```text
exp(beta U^* h U) = U^* exp(beta h) U.
```

Therefore

```text
(exp(beta h_bdy)-I)^(-1)
  = U_coex^* (exp(beta h_br)-I)^(-1) U_coex.
```

Uniqueness on one side is equivalent to uniqueness on the other side because
`U_coex` is an isometry between the carriers.

Chain: Paper 26 v2.0 Lemma C2.2 + elementary spectral functional calculus +
Lemma 26.C2c.L1.

Status: `DERIVED/THEOREM`.

Proof. Lemma 26.C2c.L2 fixes the boundary shell exponent

Chain: Paper 26 v2.0 Lemma C2.1 + Paper 26 v2.0 Lemma C2.2 + Paper 23 Theorem

## What Does Not Close

The theorem above does not prove the physical state-selection sentence:

```text
the Paper 26 scalar bridge source state is the Hawking/KMS quasi-free state on
the C2.2 coexact carrier.
```

That is the remaining C2c selection. The obstruction is structural, not a
missing line of algebra.

Reason: a CCR algebra on a fixed carrier admits many states. KMS uniqueness says
"given this dynamics and this beta, the gauge-invariant quasi-free KMS covariance
is unique." It does not say the physical state must be KMS, gauge-invariant,
quasi-free, unsqueezed, undisplaced, or generated by the Paper 22 spatial
Hamiltonian. Standard Schwarzschild QFT also distinguishes multiple physically
recognized state choices (e.g. Boulware, Unruh, Hartle-Hawking, and excited
Hadamard states). Hawking 1975 fixes the horizon temperature for Hawking
radiation; it does not by itself select the scalar bridge covariance used in
Paper 26.

Paper 25 v2.0 does not remove this obstruction. Its Theorem 25.12/25.13 package
uses the analogous H1/H2 physical-state identification:

```text
H1: Paper 22 Construction 1 + Paper 23 Lemma 23.A spatial KMS extension is the
    physical bridge state.
H2: the minimal spatial CCR lift is the physical perturbation sector.
```

The Paper 25 v2.0 bundle records `R(gamma)=1` as
`DERIVED/CONDITIONAL_VERIFIED on H1 and H2`, not as an unconditional theorem.
Therefore transporting the Paper 25 KMS uniqueness mechanism to Paper 26 closes
only the fixed-extension statement, not the bare physical selection.
